Weekly Recap

Week 5 Recap (2026-10-26 — 2026-11-01)

Monday, November 2, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Dale Prychitko

armdawg took the closest and most turbulent matchup of the week, 224.8-220.3 over I Walk for Sea Shanties. armdawg led the first three days, then I Walk for Sea Shanties pulled in front on Thursday and held the lead through Saturday night at 194.4-186.3. Sunday turned it around, and armdawg finished on top. Cutter Gauthier led the roster with 36.3 points, and Connor McDavid added 27.0. I Walk for Sea Shanties got 27.0 points and a shutout from Pyotr Kochetkov, plus 105 saves as a team, and it still came up short. That's the kind of week that sends a manager back through the lineup card looking for one more start.

Hammer beat Kegstands 234.0-223.0 after trailing for most of the week. Kegstands led from Monday through Friday, and at one point the gap was 23 points. By Friday night it was 154.0-153.9 for Kegstands, and Hammer edged in front on Saturday at 206.6-206.3. Viktor Arvidsson led Hammer with 30.3 points, and Seth Jarvis and Erik Karlsson also contributed. Kegstands put up 69 hits on the week, and Zach Benson's 29.5 points were the best on that side, but it wasn't enough.

dMC beat Slew Foot Slim 279.9-237.3, a 42.7-point margin. dMC scored 22 goals and 49 points, with Auston Matthews, Lucas Raymond and Artemi Panarin the top three scorers. Slew Foot Slim got 29.0 points from Filip Forsberg and 25.5 from Quinn Hughes, plus 95 saves, and still finished well back.

Sheeting Carlsson posted the week's best total, 297.0-205.5 over zzz, and led from Monday to Sunday. Logan Cooley (UTA) led the roster at 31.8 points, and Leo Carlsson and Joel Farabee each added 29.5. The team also logged 52 blocked shots and 67 hits. zzz had 139 saves and Evan Bouchard's 26.8 points, but only 5 goals on the week.

Beef beat Walter White Walker 259.5-176.4 with no goaltending points at all. The stat line shows no wins, saves or goals against for Beef. Their forwards and defence did the work: 130 shots, 82 hits, and 23.5 points each from Matt Coronato and Seth Jones, with Neal Pionk at 22.8. Walter White Walker had 153 saves, but they also allowed 26 goals, and Roman Josi's 17.8 points led the roster.

MazerReed beat HabsNation 249.4-167.4, and Tage Thompson (BUF) supplied 42.3 points, the biggest individual total on this week's slate. MazerReed also had a shutout in the goalie column and 127 saves. HabsNation managed only 12 assists, and its 70 hits couldn't make up the difference.

Charles beat Corey's Crazy Team 233.6-162.0, leading from the opening day. Charles had four goalie wins and 151 saves, while Corey's Crazy Team had no wins and 70 saves. That goaltending gap decided the week. Jesper Wallstedt (MIN) had 19.2 points, and Brock Boeser led the roster at 20.8.

Players of the Week

by Dale Prychitko

MazerReed gets the skater crown this week thanks to Tage Thompson. The BUF centre finished with 42.3 fantasy points on five goals, three assists, 18 shots on goal, three blocks and a hit, and every one of those points came while he was in the lineup. Thompson has always had the big shot and the big frame, and this week he used both. Cutter Gauthier of ANA was runner-up for armdawg with 36.3 points on five goals, an assist and 19 shots, plus three hits and a block. Logan Cooley of UTA gave Sheeting Carlsson 31.8 points on five goals, an assist, 10 shots and three hits. Viktor Arvidsson of DET rounded out the top four at 30.3 for Hammer, with four goals, two assists and 12 shots. Five-goal weeks were the theme at the top.

In goal, Pyotr Kochetkov of CAR takes the honour for I Walk for Sea Shanties with 27.0 points. He stopped 65 of 68 shots for a .956 save percentage, won three times and added a shutout. That shutout bonus was the difference. Plenty of goalies stop pucks, and fewer of them finish a game with nothing against. Kochetkov has been good enough in Carolina before, and this week he gave his manager the best goalie line in the division.

The runners-up were close behind. Jacob Fowler of MTL gave MazerReed 24.3 points on 71 saves, two wins and a shutout of his own, which is a fine way for a young goalie to announce himself. Jesper Wallstedt of MIN gave Charles 19.2 points on 74 saves in two wins, so his total came mostly from volume. Yaroslav Askarov of SJ gave Sheeting Carlsson 17.1 points, with a .954 save percentage and a win. Add Cooley and that makes two players on the week's honour roll for Sheeting Carlsson.

Power Rankings

by Dale Prychitko

Contenders

The top of the division is crowded, and three teams sit at 4-1-0 behind an unbeaten leader. Points for separates them more than the record does.

#1 dMC — 5-0-0 (1.000) — W, 279.9-237.3

Five wins and the division's best points-for total at 1205.0. A 237.3-point opponent would beat most teams in a given week, and dMC still found a way past it.

#2 Beef — 4-1-0 (.800) — W, 259.5-176.4

Beef is a hair behind dMC in points for at 1194.6 and has faced the second-lightest schedule of the contenders, with 993.1 against. The 83.1-point margin this week was the kind of win a title team collects.

#3 armdawg — 4-1-0 (.800) — W, 224.8-220.3

A 224.8-220.3 escape is still a win, and armdawg has four of them on the season. Its 1178.3 points for says the record is earned, though a week like this one leaves little room for error.

#4 MazerReed — 4-1-0 (.800) — W, 249.4-167.4

MazerReed has the stingiest schedule in the division at 964.4 points against, so the 4-1-0 record owes something to the draw. The 249.4 this week was the real thing, and it dropped HabsNation by 82.0.

Solid

Winning teams with questions attached. Each is either riding a soft week or sitting below where its points say it should.

#5 Charles — 3-2-0 (.600) — W, 233.6-162.0

Charles has 1105.6 points for, fifth-best in the division on my count, and the record is backing it up. Beating Corey's Crazy Team by 71.6 was a tidy week's work.

#6 Hammer — 3-2-0 (.600) — W, 234.0-223.0

Hammer took the narrow one over Kegstands by 11.0 and sits 3-2-0, but 1029.7 points for is a modest total. The record is running a little ahead of the scoring.

#7 Sheeting Carlsson — 2-3-0 (.400) — W, 297.0-205.5

Nearly 300 points is a week to build on, and Sheeting Carlsson had it against zzz with 297.0. At 2-3-0 the record lags the 1069.5 points for, so this team ranks above its standing.

Mediocre

The middle of the pack, where one good week or one bad goalie decides which way a team drifts.

#8 Corey's Crazy Team — 3-2-0 (.600) — L, 162.0-233.6

Corey's Crazy Team has three wins but only 1003.5 points for, and a 162.0 week against Charles does nothing for the case. The record is carrying more weight than the scoring.

#9 Slew Foot Slim — 2-3-0 (.400) — L, 237.3-279.9

Losing with 237.3 points is bad luck, since only four teams in the division scored more this week. Slew Foot Slim has 1065.8 points for and deserves a better record than 2-3-0.

#10 zzz — 2-3-0 (.400) — L, 205.5-297.0

zzz has faced 1151.6 points against, the second-heaviest schedule in the division by my read of the numbers, and Sheeting Carlsson added to it this week. The 1043.2 points for suggests a team that is better than 2-3-0.

#11 Kegstands — 2-3-0 (.400) — L, 223.0-234.0

Kegstands lost by 11.0 to Hammer on a week when 223.0 would have beaten plenty of opponents. With 1017.4 points for and 1108.7 against, the schedule has not been kind.

Rebuilding

Three teams with a combined one win between them, though the reasons differ.

#12 HabsNation — 1-4-0 (.200) — L, 167.4-249.4

HabsNation is 1-4-0 with 909.8 points for, and the 167.4 this week gave MazerReed an easy one. Not much is going right at the moment.

#13 I Walk for Sea Shanties — 0-5-0 (.000) — L, 220.3-224.8

I Walk for Sea Shanties lost 224.8-220.3 to armdawg, the closest result of the week. The 928.2 points for is thin, but a winless record is partly bad luck on a week like this one.

#14 Walter White Walker — 0-5-0 (.000) — L, 176.4-259.5

Walter White Walker has 895.0 points for, the lowest in the division, and 1200.8 against, the highest. Those two numbers together explain the 0-5-0 start: a weak roster, and the schedule piling on besides.

Movers and Shakers

by Dale Prychitko

Hammer and Sheeting Carlsson made the biggest climbs this week, each up three spots. Hammer now sits sixth at 3-2-0, which puts it in the middle of a tight pack. Sheeting Carlsson reached eighth at 2-3-0. The one-spot gains were Beef, now second at 4-1-0, and Charles, up to fifth at 3-2-0. I Walk for Sea Shanties also moved up one, to 13th, though at 0-5-0 that is a small comfort.

The slide was just as steep for zzz, which dropped three spots to tenth at 2-3-0. Corey's Crazy Team fell two to seventh at 3-2-0, and armdawg slipped one to third despite a 4-1-0 record that matches Beef. Slew Foot Slim (ninth) and Kegstands (11th) each gave up a spot and both stand at 2-3-0. Four teams share that record, so the order from eighth to 11th could shuffle again next week.

At the bottom, Walter White Walker fell to 14th at 0-5-0. It shares that record with I Walk for Sea Shanties, and a first win would help either team a great deal. Five games in, a few points in a single week can still move a team a long way up or down.

Best Pickup of the Week

by Dale Prychitko

Charles wins Best Pickup of the Week with Kasperi Kapanen. The EDM right winger put up 18.3 fantasy points on one goal, two assists, seven shots, a blocked shot and 15 hits. That hit total is the kind of unglamorous production that adds up quickly at 0.25 a pop. Kapanen has bounced around the league for years as a player with speed and a lot of promise, so a quiet bit of depth scoring on the Oilers' wing is a good reminder of what the free-agent pool can offer. Credit to the manager for spotting it.

The runners-up both belong to dMC, which grabbed two Ottawa forwards and got something from each. Ridly Greig delivered 12.3 points with a goal, a shorthanded point, six blocks and five hits. Claude Giroux, the veteran who keeps finding ways to contribute, added 10.3 points on a goal, an assist and three shots. Two useful adds from one roster, and the pair of them cleared ten points apiece.

Hall of Shame

by Dale Prychitko

Not every pickup works out, and this week's crop of duds proves it. Walter White Walker takes top billing for Barrett Hayton, the UTA centre who finished the week with one shot on goal and 0.5 fantasy points. That is a roster spot that produced about what a shot and a shrug produce. Hayton has the pedigree of a former high pick, but pedigree does not score in this league.

HabsNation brought in Ryker Evans, the SEA defenceman, who managed 1.8 points on a shot, a hit and two blocks. A blue-liner who blocks shots can pay off, but not at this volume. Beef rounds out the group with Mason Marchment, the SJ left winger, who put up 2.8 points on two shots, three hits and two blocks, all without a goal or assist while in the lineup. Busy, yes. Productive, not especially.

The most telling part is how thin the numbers are across all three. Between them, those three pickups never recorded a goal or assist while in the lineup, and the best of the lot finished under three fantasy points. A pickup that does not move the needle is a lost roster spot, and the managers know it.

Goalie Stream of the Week

by Dale Prychitko

Charles gets the nod for Goalie Stream of the Week with Akira Schmid of FLA, who finished with 15.3 fantasy points. The line is a clean one: two wins, 46 saves on 49 shots against, three goals against and a .939 save percentage. No shutout, but two wins in a week is the headline, because at three points apiece they carry a large share of the total. Saves did the rest of the work, and Schmid stopped nearly everything he saw.

It was a good week for Charles all around, since the same manager also landed the top pickup in Kasperi Kapanen. A manager who is finding value among both the free-agent skaters and goalies is paying attention.

Kegstands takes second with Anthony Stolarz of TOR, who earned 7.8 points. He picked up one win and stopped 26 of 28 shots for a .929 save percentage. Solid work, and a fine return for a streaming bet, though it landed at about half of what Schmid delivered.

Front Office Failures

by Dale Prychitko

The biggest bench misstep belongs to Sheeting Carlsson, who left Shane Pinto on the pine on two days when he had stats. Pinto finished with two goals, six shots and three hits for 12.8 fantasy points across those days, and every one of them sat idle. That is a lot of production to leave off the scoresheet. Sheeting Carlsson also dropped Dylan Garand, who was promptly picked up by Beef, so the manager managed to feed a divisional rival as well.

Corey's Crazy Team made the mistake twice, benching Anthony Cirelli for 11.3 points on a goal and two assists, and Sebastian Aho for 10.0 points on a goal, an assist and a shorthanded point. Beef also sat Mason Marchment on a day he scored a goal and an assist for 9.0 points, which is a puzzling note given that he was a fresh free-agent add.

Finally, dMC is still rostering Carter Hart, who has a .867 save percentage over the last two weeks on 98 shots against. At some point a goalie's track record has to give way to the current numbers.

Transaction Desk

by Dale Prychitko

The big trade of the week sent Sidney Crosby to armdawg and Bo Horvat to Slew Foot Slim. Crosby is a legend whose scoring has held up well into his late career. I give the nod to armdawg here, since Crosby is the more productive asset and Horvat is a fine but lesser option for the Islanders.

The earlier deal on October 27 was more of a lateral swap. armdawg picked up defenceman Bowen Byram from CHI, who is listed as day-to-day, while Sheeting Carlsson received Cole Perfetti of WPG. Byram's injury status means he is not an immediate source of points, and Perfetti is the one playing right now. I lean toward Sheeting Carlsson in the short term, though armdawg is clearly active on the trade market, with two deals in a week.

On the FAAB side, only two bids went through, totalling $21. MazerReed spent $13 on Jonathan Lekkerimäki of VAN and got 7.0 points from an assist, five shots and four hits. Kegstands put in $8 for Michael Brandsegg-Nygård of DET, dropping Tommy Novak, and 20 hits, a shot and a block produced 6.0 points. Both are modest returns for the money, though neither bid broke the bank.

Budgets are worth watching. dMC has just $37 left, the lowest in the division, followed by zzz at $43 and armdawg at $58. Four teams, including Beef and Corey's Crazy Team, have yet to spend a dollar, which leaves them well positioned for later in the season.

The Two-Phone Report

by Frankie "Two Phones" Lombardi

Tom Wilson is the name making the rounds this week, and I'm told the phones haven't stopped. Several managers have been sniffing around the Washington power forward, and the answer coming back is the same every time: he is off the block. The only way he moves is a truly groundbreaking offer, and nobody I've spoken to has seen one. Hearing that his owner will not sell low, and a player who piles up hits, shots and blocks in a points league is not a piece anyone parts with cheaply. Keep an eye on this one.

My sources won't say who is doing the sniffing. A physical, high-volume winger is exactly the sort of piece a contender or a desperate manager chases. But the 24-hour counter-offer window means any deal for him would draw a bidding war from the rest of the division the moment it's accepted. If a deal gets done, a counter offer is coming. I'd bet on it.

The deals that did get done tell the real story. armdawg has been the busiest shop in the division, picking up Bowen Byram from Sheeting Carlsson for Cole Perfetti. Then armdawg landed Sidney Crosby from Slew Foot Slim in exchange for Bo Horvat. Two trades, two roster-shaping moves, and armdawg sits at 4-1-0 in a tie for second. Is this a contender going all in on star power? Or a manager who spotted two sellers and took advantage? Both Slew Foot Slim and Sheeting Carlsson are sitting at 2-3-0, and both made moves that read like retooling on the fly.

On the wire, MazerReed put in a $13 bid on Jonathan Lekkerimäki, a prospect-pedigree scoring winger that a 4-1-0 team can afford to stash. Kegstands went for Michael Brandsegg-Nygård at $8, a modest bid from a 2-3-0 club still looking for traction. Neither is a headline, but both tell me who is thinking about next month and not just next week.

Meanwhile, dMC is the lone unbeaten team at 5-0-0, and everyone knows the target on that back grows with every win. The trade deadline is February 17, and the countdown clock is already ticking in my head. When the pressure builds, the phones will ring. Mine already are.

Expected Points

by Priya Raman

Beef is the week's cleanest argument for goaltending as variance. They led the division in shots on goal (130) and hits (82) and scored 259.5 points, third on the board behind Sheeting Carlsson's 297.0 and dMC's 279.9. The goalie line is empty: zero wins, zero goals against, zero saves. Skaters alone beat Walter White Walker 259.5-176.4, and the opponent's goalies made 153 saves, the division high.

That matchup is the week's best illustration of how little saves buy without the rest. Walter White Walker also allowed 26 goals against, the most in the division, and lost by a wide margin anyway. Charles went the other way: just 76 shots on goal and 12 goals, but 151 saves and four wins, tied for the division high, for a 233.6-162.0 win over Corey's Crazy Team. The goalies carried that one. Corey's Crazy Team finished with 162.0, the division low, and went winless in net.

Slew Foot Slim was the unluckiest team of the week: 237.3 points, fifth-best in the division, and a loss. I Walk for Sea Shanties also lost a close one, 224.8-220.3 to armdawg, despite one of the week's two shutouts (MazerReed had the other). Their goalies allowed 8 goals against on 105 saves, a strong line, while armdawg gave up 16 and won on skater volume: 110 shots, 24 assists, 15 goals.

dMC scored the most goals (22) and points (49, tied with Sheeting Carlsson), and finished with 279.9 fantasy points, second in the division behind only Sheeting Carlsson. The weak spot was net: 63 saves, the second-lowest in the division, and one win. A normal goalie week on top of that skater line would have pushed dMC toward the top. Skater scoring this strong is hard to repeat at 22 goals, and the goaltending has room to improve, so those two forces point in opposite directions next week.
