Weekly Recap

Week 6 Recap (2026-11-02 — 2026-11-08)

Monday, November 9, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Dale Prychitko

Beef took the closest and wildest matchup of the division, 248.0-235.9 over MazerReed. Beef led through Wednesday night, then MazerReed went in front on Thursday and held the lead through Saturday, 217.7-210.0 at the close of play. Sunday flipped it. Tomas Hertl led the Beef roster with 35.3 points, with Connor Bedard at 25.8 and Kyle Connor at 22.3. Beef finished the week with 20 goals and 131 shots on goal and without a single save from its goalies, so the skaters carried everything. Leon Draisaitl gave MazerReed 35.0 points, but that wasn't enough to hold the lead.

dMC beat Charles 255.5-221.2 in a seesaw that changed hands twice. Charles took the lead on Tuesday, 97.9-81.8, and dMC moved back in front on Wednesday and stayed there the rest of the way. Beckett Sennecke of ANA led the dMC roster with 39.5 points, with Sam Reinhart at 26.8 and Carter Verhaeghe at 22.8, which made for a good week for the Florida forwards. Jason Robertson (27.5) and Clayton Keller (26.3) kept Charles in it for most of the week.

Kegstands edged Walter White Walker 242.6-223.8, and the lead changed hands twice on the way. Walter White Walker got the better goaltending, with 8 wins, 231 saves and a shutout on the week, and Connor Hellebuyck had 22.2 points. Kegstands answered with skaters: 14 goals to 9, and 101 shots to 84. Kirill Kaprizov (24.3), Adrian Kempe (23.0) and Tristan Luneau (21.5) led the way. Nathan MacKinnon had 25.3 points and Roman Josi 23.5 for the losers.

zzz beat Slew Foot Slim 267.2-236.7 in another two-lead-change game. Slew Foot Slim was in front after Tuesday and Wednesday, and then zzz took over and kept pulling away. The goaltending made the difference. zzz had 6 wins, 194 saves and a shutout, while Slew Foot Slim had no wins and 104 saves. Jack Hughes of NJ gave Slew Foot Slim 42.5 points, the biggest individual line in that game. Mark Scheifele led zzz with 26.8 points, with Martin Necas and Andrei Svechnikov close behind.

armdawg beat Corey's Crazy Team 271.5-241.0, and Cutter Gauthier of ANA did most of it. His 52.3 points were the biggest single-player total in any of the seven matchups, and Connor McDavid added 33.0. Corey's Crazy Team played well at forward, with Macklin Celebrini (30.3), Jesper Bratt (28.0) and Sebastian Aho (21.8) all producing. It finished the week with no goalie wins and only 25 saves, and that gap cost it the matchup.

Hammer beat I Walk for Sea Shanties 271.7-204.3 and never trailed. Mikael Granlund of ANA led the roster with 40.8 points, with Brady Tkachuk at 32.8 and Cale Makar at 28.3. Hammer had 80 hits on the week and 3 shorthanded points, the kind of unglamorous production that adds up quickly. Jackson LaCombe gave I Walk for Sea Shanties 30.0 points, but the rest of the roster couldn't keep pace.

Sheeting Carlsson beat HabsNation 267.1-151.5, the most lopsided result of the week. HabsNation managed 19 points on the week, with 7 goals and 12 assists. Mitch Marner led the roster with 25.3 fantasy points and Andrei Vasilevskiy added 15.3. Sheeting Carlsson put up 18 goals and 124 shots, and Thomas Harley (26.5), Leo Carlsson (24.8) and Logan Cooley (24.5) all produced. There wasn't much suspense once the week got going.

Players of the Week

by Dale Prychitko

Cutter Gauthier takes the skater crown, and it wasn't close. The Anaheim winger-slash-centre piled up 8 goals, 2 assists and 19 shots on goal for 52.3 fantasy points, nearly ten clear of anyone else in the division. Gauthier was the fifth overall pick in 2022 and has spent his short career proving that Philadelphia gave up on the wrong kid. Eight goals is a month of production for most wingers, and armdawg got all of it in one matchup week. Credit to Armin for holding a player who is eligible at C, LW and RW and can slot wherever the lineup needs him.

The runners-up were a crowd. Jack Hughes of NJ put up 42.5 fantasy points for Slew Foot Slim on 3 goals, 6 assists and 19 shots, and the shot volume tells you the Devils' centre is healthy and driving. Mikael Granlund of ANA came in at 40.8 for Hammer, with 10 points, a shorthanded point and 4 blocks, the sort of all-around line that makes a veteran playmaker worth rostering. Beckett Sennecke, also of ANA, finished at 39.5 for dMC with 2 goals and 8 assists. Three Ducks in the top four skaters says plenty about how that club's power play treated the fantasy world this week.

In goal, Connor Hellebuyck of NJ wins it for Walter White Walker with 22.2 fantasy points on 3 wins, a shutout and 69 saves. His .908 save percentage won't make a highlight reel, but the volume of 76 shots against and the wins carried the day. Mackenzie Blackwood of COL was a hair behind at 21.6 for zzz on 82 saves, a shutout and 2 wins, and Morgan had a second goalie in the mix as well: Jeremy Swayman of BOS posted 19.8 on a .953 save percentage. Juuse Saros of NSH rounds out the group at 19.2 for Kegstands, with just 2 goals against on 56 shots. Four goalies within three points of each other tells you the position stayed mostly orderly this week.

Power Rankings

by Dale Prychitko

Contenders

Four teams sit at the top of Clouston, and the gap between them and the rest of the pack is the points-for column as much as the standings.

#1 dMC — 6-0-0 (1.000) — W, 255.5-221.2

Six wins from six, with 1460.4 points for, tops the table and the points column both. Charles led after Tuesday, but dMC took it back on Wednesday and never gave it up.

#2 armdawg — 5-1-0 (.833) — W, 271.5-241.0

The second-highest score of the week, 271.5, beat Corey's Crazy Team, and the 1449.8 points for are only a shade behind the leader. Only Hammer scored more this week.

#3 Beef — 5-1-0 (.833) — W, 248.0-235.9

A 12.1-point win over MazerReed keeps them level with armdawg on record. Their 1229.0 points against is among the lowest in the division, so the schedule has done them a few favours.

#4 MazerReed — 4-2-0 (.667) — L, 235.9-248.0

They lost by 12.1 points to Beef, and 235.9 is a score that wins plenty of weeks. At 1393.5 points for, this team is a contender with a bad Sunday on its ledger.

Solid

Three teams with winning or even records and enough scoring to be dangerous, though none of them has shown it every night.

#5 Sheeting Carlsson — 3-3-0 (.500) — W, 267.1-151.5

Their 267.1 was the fourth-highest score of the week, and HabsNation was the one on the other end of it. At .500 with 1336.6 points for, the record undersells the roster.

#6 Hammer — 4-2-0 (.667) — W, 271.7-204.3

The highest score in the division this week, and it came against I Walk for Sea Shanties. The season total of 1301.4 points for is modest next to the teams above, so this reads as one big week rather than a trend.

#7 Charles — 3-3-0 (.500) — L, 221.2-255.5

They fell 34.3 points short of dMC, but 1326.7 points for is better than the record suggests. Another 1290.0 points against shows the schedule hasn't been kind either.

Mediocre

Four teams circling .500, and in a points league that usually means the schedule is doing some of the talking.

#8 zzz — 3-3-0 (.500) — W, 267.2-236.7

The third-highest score of the week, 267.2, beat Slew Foot Slim. Their 1388.3 points against is the sign of a team that has drawn some big weeks and deserves a better record than .500.

#9 Kegstands — 3-3-0 (.500) — W, 242.6-223.8

A win over Walter White Walker that settled nothing about this team's ceiling. They are living in the middle of the table with 1259.9 points for.

#10 Corey's Crazy Team — 3-3-0 (.500) — L, 241.0-271.5

A 241.0 would win a good many weeks, but it ran into armdawg on a big night. The record has taken the hit more than the roster has.

#11 Slew Foot Slim — 2-4-0 (.333) — L, 236.7-267.2

Points for and points against sit almost level at 1302.5 and 1302.4, which is the profile of a .500 team wearing a 2-4-0 record. A few more nights like this one against zzz, though, and the record will be fair.

Rebuilding

Three teams still looking for traction, and one of them has the scoring to climb out sooner than the standings suggest.

#12 HabsNation — 1-5-0 (.167) — L, 151.5-267.1

The 151.5 was the lowest score in the division this week, and Sheeting Carlsson took full advantage. With 1061.3 points for, the lineup needs work before the standings can move.

#13 I Walk for Sea Shanties — 0-6-0 (.000) — L, 204.3-271.7

They ran into Hammer on its best night of the year, which is hard luck at 204.3. The 1132.5 points for tells the same story as the record, though.

#14 Walter White Walker — 0-6-0 (.000) — L, 223.8-242.6

A 223.8 lost by 18.8 points to Kegstands, which fits a season of unlucky draws. They have faced 1443.3 points against, so some of this is the schedule, and the points-for column at 1118.8 says the rest is the roster.

Movers and Shakers

by Dale Prychitko

armdawg and Beef both sit at 5-1-0, and the order flipped this week. armdawg climbed one spot to #2 while Beef slid one to #3. Hammer also moved up a place to #5 at 4-2-0. Nobody at the top is in danger yet, but the gap between the 5-1-0 pair and the rest of the division is real.

The real churn is in the middle, where five teams are bunched at 3-3-0 and the order changes every Monday. Sheeting Carlsson and zzz and Kegstands each gained two spots, landing at #6, #8 and #9. Charles dropped two to #7, and Corey's Crazy Team took the worst of it, falling three places to #10. Slew Foot Slim also lost two spots and sits at #11 with a 2-4-0 record. A single big week will reshuffle that 3-3-0 group again, so nobody there should get comfortable.

Best Pickup of the Week

by Dale Prychitko

Hammer had two of the three best pickups of the week, but the top spot goes to MazerReed and Jamie Drysdale. The PHI defenceman gave MazerReed 15.3 fantasy points in three games, all in the active lineup. He had three assists, five shots on goal and seven blocked shots. Seven blocks at half a point each is the sort of unglamorous production that wins a points-league week while everyone else watches the goal scorers. Drysdale has always had the offensive instincts, and on a PHI blue line that gets him power-play minutes he now has the role to use them. Good pickup, well timed.

Martin Pospisil took second for Hammer with 13.5 points. The CGY forward is eligible at centre and right wing and had 22 hits in four games. A hit is only a quarter point, but 22 of them add up faster than anyone expects, and he threw in a shorthanded point on top. I once watched a fourth-line winger in Melfort do exactly this for a whole winter. Nobody cheered him, and the standings looked better for him.

Tommy Novak rounds out the list, also for Hammer, with two goals and 12.3 points for PIT in three games. Two of the top three pickups came from one roster, which says someone in Hammer's corner is paying attention to the wire. That's worth noting, and the rest of the division should check their own waiver habits.

Hall of Shame

by Dale Prychitko

Nobody in this week's Hall of Shame lost money on the pickup, and that is the problem. These three finished in the bottom tier of the week's adds, and none of them was a catastrophe. They were all just a bit underwhelming.

HabsNation gets top billing for Michael Misa. The young SJ centre scored a goal and put three shots on net in two games, which came to 6.8 fantasy points. It's a decent line for a prospect finding his feet, but the club has a limit on adds per week and a spot that could have gone to a hit-and-block machine. Misa may well turn into something special in San Jose, but this week he earned a seat in the Hall of Shame by default.

Slew Foot Slim picked up Drew Doughty of LA and got 7.3 points over two games: an assist, five hits and four blocks. That's honest work from a veteran defenceman who has been through more hockey than most of this division has watched. Real contribution, just not much of one. Corey's Crazy Team added Justin Barron of STL, who had six shots and three hits but only one assist in three games for 8.3 points. That line lands at the very top of this list, which tells you what kind of week it was for the wire. Pickups like these keep a roster ticking along. They do not win a week, and the managers should know the difference.

Goalie Stream of the Week

by Dale Prychitko

dMC gets the nod this week, and the margin was slim. Ukko-Pekka Luukkonen of BUF made 24 saves on 26 shots, took the win and finished with 7.2 fantasy points. He allowed two goals and posted a .923 save percentage. The win, the saves and the goals against all told the same story: a solid night in a game that didn't ask much more of him.

Sheeting Carlsson was close behind with Dylan Garand of NYR, who stopped 23 of 25 for a .920 and a win. That produced 6.9 points. It's a half-step behind Luukkonen and the shape of the line is nearly identical, so the gap comes down to a couple of saves.

Walter White Walker took the third spot with Joonas Korpisalo, another NYR netminder, who won while stopping 25 of 28. Three goals against and a .893 save percentage held him to 6.0 points. He got the win, which is the part of a stream that matters most, but the goals against ate into the total. Two different NYR goalies landing in the top three tells me somebody on Broadway's crease has been busy this week, and the managers who noticed are the ones who cashed in.

Front Office Failures

by Dale Prychitko

The bench is where good managers go to lose matchups, and HabsNation is the cautionary tale of the week. Steven Stamkos sat on the pine for two days when he had stats, and in that stretch he scored a goal, added an assist and fired eight shots on goal for 12.5 fantasy points. Those are points that never made it into the matchup total. This is the same roster that picked up Michael Misa and dropped Luke Evangelista, who then put up 1.5 points for MazerReed. When a manager is busy on the wire, the lineup is where mistakes happen.

Charles has two entries and a headache. Zachary Bolduc sat on the bench the entire week while producing a goal, seven shots, five hits and a block for 9.8 points. Jesper Wallstedt also sat on one day when he had stats, and he stopped 23 of 24 shots for the win and 8.4 points. That's a lot of production left on the bench from one manager, and I'd say the bench slots deserved a closer look before the weekend.

The rest of the list is smaller but still stings. armdawg left J.T. Miller on the bench for a day when he had two assists, four shots and two blocks, worth 9.3 points. Walter White Walker benched Adam Edstrom for a day when he scored and threw eight hits, worth 8.0 points. And I Walk for Sea Shanties dropped James Hagens, who was claimed by Corey's Crazy Team for a hefty bid. Letting a young BOS forward walk for nothing and watching him fetch $50 from a rival stings a little.

Transaction Desk

by Dale Prychitko

Only one bid mattered at the FAAB window this week, and Corey's Crazy Team made it. They put $50 on James Hagens, the young BOS forward eligible at centre and both wings, and dropped Anthony Cirelli to make room. That is half the starting budget on a single player, a bold swing on a prospect with a high ceiling. Hagens was dropped by I Walk for Sea Shanties, who now sit on a full $100 and can watch from the sidelines to see whether their former player turns into something. The spend leaves Corey's Crazy Team with $50 remaining. Whether it was justified depends on how soon Hagens shows up in the box score. A half-budget bid on a player who's still finding his role is a gamble, and I'd want to see a few more games before calling it smart.

Total spending this week was $50, and all of it came from that one bid. Everyone else either sat out or picked up free agents. That leaves dMC with the lowest remaining budget at $37, followed by zzz at $43. armdawg has $58, Sheeting Carlsson has $61 and Hammer has $72. Those managers have been busy on the wire, and their budgets show it.

At the other end, three teams still hold a full $100: Beef, HabsNation and I Walk for Sea Shanties. Walter White Walker sits at $99, Charles at $94, Kegstands at $92, Slew Foot Slim at $89 and MazerReed at $84. A healthy war chest is good, but unspent dollars win nothing, and with the trade deadline still months away, some of these managers will want to put that money to work before an injury forces their hand.

The Two-Phone Report

by Frankie "Two Phones" Lombardi

I'm told Beef is working the phones, and the 5-1-0 record makes it more interesting. Sources say the shop is open on some of their defencemen, with the asking price being forward help. A contender sitting third in the standings that wants to turn blue-line depth into scoring up front is a team that sees a gap and has decided to close it before anyone else does. I'm hearing the list of interested parties is longer than Beef expected.

The part that made me put down my coffee: Gavin McKenna might be available too. Beef made the move for him earlier this season, so a quick flip raises eyebrows. Either the young forward hasn't delivered on the price paid, or Beef sees a bigger fish and needs a chip to land it. Keep an eye on that one, because a manager who sells a prospect that fast has usually already lined up the next call.

Now to Sheeting Carlsson, who are 3-3-0 and sitting squarely in the middle of the pack. I'm told they want to make a move, and the whole roster is on the table with two exceptions: Matt Boldy and Dylan Guenther are off limits. Those two are the core, and everything around them is fair game. A .500 team that wants to shake things up is either a buyer in disguise or a seller waiting for the right offer. With the February 17 deadline on the calendar, I'd expect that clock to start ticking loudly soon.

Put the two together and connect the dots, because I certainly have. Beef has defence to move and wants forwards. Sheeting Carlsson has pieces to move and wants something. Whether those two sit down at the same table, nobody can say, but the Discord channel is going to be busy. And when that 24-hour counter-offer window opens on an accepted deal, I'll have eyes on it.

One more note from the wire. Corey's Crazy Team put in a $50 bid on James Hagens, half the FAAB budget on one young player. That is a statement from a 3-3-0 club, and it tells me Corey's Crazy Team is thinking about next year while the rest of the division is thinking about this week. Watch whether that bid is a one-off or the first sign of a bigger shopping spree.

Expected Points

by Priya Raman

Beef won with a goalie line of zeros: no wins, no saves, no shutouts and no goals against. The 0 in GA is the league's best figure only because nothing was recorded. The skaters carried it, with a league-best 131 shots on goal, 20 goals and 60 hits. That beat MazerReed, 248.0-235.9. MazerReed got 83 saves and one win from its goalies, which made the matchup a test of whether volume shooting can replace goaltending. This week it could.

Corey's Crazy Team has the week's best argument for a recount. Its 241.0 points beat six of the other thirteen teams, yet it lost to armdawg, 271.5-241.0. All seven winners scored at least 242.6, so the losing side of the ledger was crowded with respectable totals. Corey's Crazy Team had 124 shots and 44 points, but its goalies recorded only 25 saves and no wins. armdawg led the league with 21 goals and 46 points and also got 150 saves. A team can do almost everything right and still draw the one opponent who did slightly more.

Walter White Walker is the week's goaltending cautionary tale. It led the league with 8 wins, 231 saves and a shutout, and it also posted the league's highest goals against at 32. The skaters then gave back whatever the crease produced: 84 shots against a league average of 105.57, and 30 points. The result was a 223.8-242.6 loss to Kegstands, whose goalies had only 131 saves and 4 wins. zzz shows the better version of the same bet. It had 194 saves, 6 wins and a shutout, with 16 goals from its skaters, and finished third in the weekly scoring at 267.2.

At the top, Hammer posted the week's best score at 271.7, only 0.2 ahead of armdawg. The edge came from the unglamorous stats: 80 hits against a league average of 45.93, plus 3 shorthanded points, also tops in the division. Slew Foot Slim is the reverse case. It led the league with 36 assists and 42 blocks, but its goalies produced no wins and it lost to zzz, 267.2-236.7. Goalie wins mattered, but not every week turned on them: Beef won without a single goalie start.
