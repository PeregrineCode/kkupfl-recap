Weekly Recap

Week 3 Recap (2026-10-12 — 2026-10-18)

Wednesday, October 21, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Dale Prychitko

The closest game of the week came down to a single decision. Corey's Crazy Team edged Sheeting Carlsson 194.6-192.4, a margin of 2.2 points. Sheeting Carlsson outshot them 102 to 87 and had the better skater in Matt Boldy (MIN), who led the roster with 31.0 points. Corey's Crazy Team got there with Macklin Celebrini (SJ) at 26.5 and Ryan O'Reilly (NSH) at 24.5, plus two goalie wins to one for the other side. In a points league that goalie edge decides a close week. Sheeting Carlsson gave up 13 goals against to the winner's 18 and still lost, which tells you how much the extra win was worth.

Two mid-sized results had very different shapes. Hammer beat Walter White Walker 222.8-198.7, and Brady Tkachuk (FLA) led the roster at 51.75 points, with Mark Stone (VGK) adding 30.75. Walter White Walker had the stronger goaltending, with 5 wins, 2 shutouts and 213 saves. It scored only 3 goals, though, and the skater side never kept pace. MazerReed took down zzz 230.5-202.9 on a balanced attack. Tage Thompson (BUF), Leon Draisaitl (EDM) and Tim Stutzle (OTT) all reached 20 points. zzz leaned on defencemen, with Rasmus Andersson (VGK), Lane Hutson (MTL) and Evan Bouchard (EDM) as its top three scorers, and it came up short.

Two matchups finished with the same 35.4-point margin. dMC beat HabsNation 211.6-176.2 behind Sam Reinhart (FLA), who led the roster with 41.75 points. dMC posted 18 goals, 47 hits and 45 blocks. HabsNation piled up 63 hits, but with 55 shots on goal and 6 goals the hits could not carry the week. Kegstands beat Charles 250.1-214.7, with Kirill Kaprizov (MIN) at 36.0 and Jack Quinn (BUF) at 31.0 doing the heavy lifting. Charles matched the winner with 14 goals and got 29.25 points from Jack Eichel (VGK), but 83 saves against the winner's 141 left a gap.

The two blowouts closed the slate. Beef beat armdawg 278.1-217.0, the highest total anyone posted. It fired 122 shots on goal and allowed only 6 goals against. Seth Jones (FLA) led the roster with 37.25 points as a defenceman, which is exactly the kind of unglamorous production that piles up in this format. Connor McDavid (EDM) put up 39.25 for armdawg, and it was not enough. The biggest margin belonged to Slew Foot Slim, who beat I Walk for Sea Shanties 239.8-168.3, a 71.5-point gap. Matthew Tkachuk (FLA) led with 34.5, and 31 assists across the roster kept the scoring steady. I Walk for Sea Shanties finished with the lowest total in the division, even with David Pastrnak (BOS) at 24.75.

Players of the Week

by Dale Prychitko

Brady Tkachuk of Hammer took the skater crown with 51.75 points, and nobody else came within ten. Six goals, four assists, 20 shots on goal and 11 hits on the week for FLA. That's the Tkachuk package: a power forward who gets his points from the goal crease and then adds hits on top. The 20 shots alone are a respectable week for most skaters. Add the physical play and you have a clear winner in a points format that pays for almost everything he does.

The runners-up were crowded with FLA names. Sam Reinhart of dMC put up 41.75 points on five goals and three assists, with 17 shots and two blocks, so Florida's top line had a good week for two different fantasy teams. Connor McDavid of armdawg landed at 39.25 on three goals and seven assists, which is a quiet week by his standards and still third in the division. Seth Jones of Beef rounds out the group at 37.25, and a defenceman with nine assists, 13 shots, five hits and five blocks is the kind of do-everything contributor a points league rewards.

Between the pipes, Linus Ullmark of Beef was the goalie of the week with 22.2 points. Two wins and 74 saves on 78 shots for OTT, a .949 save percentage, and only four goals against. Jeremy Swayman of zzz was next at 18 points on a .938 over two wins, and Ilya Sorokin of Slew Foot Slim posted 17.1 on a .945 with two wins of his own. Jet Greaves of Walter White Walker gets a nod too: 33 saves on 33 shots for CBJ, a shutout, and 15.9 points. That's about as efficient as a week of goaltending gets.

Power Rankings

by Dale Prychitko

Contenders

The top of the division is settled by points as much as by record. Two unbeaten teams and two with one loss each have earned the room up here.

#1 Beef — 3-0-0 (1.000) — W, 278.1-217.0

The biggest total of the week came against armdawg, and 737.8 points for on the season is the highest in the division. Unbeaten and the points leader makes this an easy call at the top.

#2 dMC — 3-0-0 (1.000) — W, 211.6-176.2

Also unbeaten, with 648.9 points for against 569.1 faced. The scoring is a step behind Beef, but a clean record built on a decent points margin holds up.

#3 armdawg — 2-1-0 (.667) — L, 217.0-278.1

Lost to the week's high scorer, and 217.0 points would have won plenty of other matchups. With 684.7 points for on the season, second only to Beef, this team stays in the top four.

#4 MazerReed — 2-1-0 (.667) — W, 230.5-202.9

A solid win over zzz keeps the record at 2-1-0. The 640.7 points for is healthy, though the 623.6 against says the schedule hasn't been soft.

Solid

These three teams all sit at 2-1-0 and all won this week. The points for are middling, and that keeps them out of the top group.

#5 Slew Foot Slim — 2-1-0 (.667) — W, 239.8-168.3

Beating I Walk for Sea Shanties by that kind of margin was the week's most lopsided result. Only 526.1 points against on the season, the lowest in the division, suggests a kind schedule, but 615.0 points for is real production.

#6 Corey's Crazy Team — 2-1-0 (.667) — W, 194.6-192.4

Won the closest matchup of the week against Sheeting Carlsson. A 194.6 doesn't scare anybody, but 612.4 points for says the team is steady enough to keep winning these.

#7 Kegstands — 2-1-0 (.667) — W, 250.1-214.7

The second-biggest total of the week lifted Kegstands over Charles. Points against of 606.0 outweigh the 590.0 points for, so the record is running a little ahead of the underlying numbers.

Mediocre

A cluster of 1-2-0 teams. Some are better than the record, and some are about what they look like.

#8 Charles — 1-2-0 (.333) — L, 214.7-250.1

A total of 214.7 was good enough in most weeks and lost to Kegstands. With 638.3 points for but 662.0 against, Charles is the unlucky one in this group and should climb.

#9 zzz — 1-2-0 (.333) — L, 202.9-230.5

The 626.0 points for is better than the record, and the 668.1 against is among the heaviest in the division. Points-against luck is dragging this team down.

#10 Hammer — 1-2-0 (.333) — W, 222.8-198.7

Got a win over Walter White Walker to stop the slide. The 584.6 points for hasn't matched the 637.2 against, so one win doesn't fix the underlying picture.

#11 HabsNation — 1-2-0 (.333) — L, 176.2-211.6

A quiet week against dMC. The 555.9 points for is low, though 576.9 against shows the schedule hasn't been the problem.

Rebuilding

Three teams at the bottom of the points table, two of them still looking for a first win.

#12 Sheeting Carlsson — 1-2-0 (.333) — L, 192.4-194.6

Lost by the thinnest of margins to Corey's Crazy Team, which is a tough way to drop a week. The 555.0 points for says the team isn't scoring enough to keep getting away with close ones.

#13 Walter White Walker — 0-3-0 (.000) — L, 198.7-222.8

Winless, but the 673.7 points against is the heaviest in the division, so some of this is schedule. The 545.2 points for still has to come up before the luck can turn.

#14 I Walk for Sea Shanties — 0-3-0 (.000) — L, 168.3-239.8

Winless, with the lowest points for in the division at 497.3, and this week's total was the lowest of the round. The 610.1 against is middling, so the schedule isn't the excuse here.

Best Pickup of the Week

by Dale Prychitko

Kegstands gets the nod this week for the best pickup on the board. Trevor Connelly (LW, VGK) came off the wire and put up 15.3 points: two goals, an assist, five shots on goal, a hit and a block. That is a full line of production from a roster spot that cost nothing but attention. Connelly was a first-round pick not long ago, and a young winger riding a top-six role in Vegas is exactly the kind of bet worth making on a Tuesday.

Runner-up honours go to Hammer, who added Easton Cowan (LW, TOR) and got 11.3 points out of him. Two assists, six shots, three hits and three blocks. That is the kind of unglamorous volume a points league pays for. Third is Charles, with Zachary Bolduc (LW,RW, MTL) at 10.8 points on a goal, an assist, five shots and three hits.

Three wingers, three useful weeks, and all three managers did their work on the waiver wire instead of waiting for the phone to ring. A man who checks the free-agent list every week will find a Connelly sooner or later.

Hall of Shame

by Dale Prychitko

The bottom of the pickup board this week is a tidy lesson in what a point or two buys a manager. Not much.

Charles takes the low mark with Fraser Minten (C, BOS), who managed 1.3 points: two shots and a hit, and nothing on the scoresheet. Next to him is HabsNation, who added Josh Anderson (RW, MTL) for 1.8 points on two shots and three hits. Anderson has made a career out of throwing his body around, and the hits are there, but without a goal or an assist behind them they only add up to a few quarters. MazerReed rounds out the list with Michael Brandsegg-Nygård (RW, DET) at 2.8 points, three shots and five hits. Five hits is honest work. It is also not much of a return on a roster spot.

The lesson is an old one: in this format, a depth forward who hits and shoots without ever finding the net is a bench player wearing a starter's sweater. None of these three will sink a season. All three should be back on the wire by next weekend.

Goalie Stream of the Week

by Dale Prychitko

Walter White Walker worked the goalie market twice this week and came away with the top stream. Alex Nedeljkovic (SJ) stopped 32 of 34 shots for a win and 9.6 points. A .941 save percentage on a San Jose team that tends to give its goalie plenty of work is a decent night. The win's three points and the saves carried it, and only two goals against dented it.

Cam Talbot (CBJ), the other Walter White Walker stream, matched the line almost exactly: 32 saves on 34 shots, .941, but no win. That left him at 6.6 points, a plain illustration of how much the win is worth in this format.

Honourable mention goes to MazerReed and Jacob Fowler (MTL). He picked up two wins and 7.8 points, but he also allowed six goals on 42 shots for a .857 save percentage. The wins carried the stream and the goals against nearly sank it. Fowler paid out, but this was the high-wire version of streaming.

Front Office Failures

by Dale Prychitko

The most telling entry in the file this week is the Jakub Dobes situation. MazerReed dropped Dobes, and Sheeting Carlsson put in a $22 claim for him, dropping Parker Kelly to make room. One manager let a goalie go and another was glad to have him, and the second manager is the one who looks sharp.

Sheeting Carlsson was on the receiving end of a lot of this week's cast-offs. Kegstands dropped Valeri Nichushkin and armdawg dropped Tyson Foerster, and both landed with Sheeting Carlsson. Nichushkin is a physical winger with real scoring pedigree when healthy, and Foerster has shown he can score when he stays in the lineup. That is two forwards cut loose and scooped up inside a week. Maybe those drops were injury-driven, but the pattern is hard to ignore.

Kegstands also dropped Adam Jiricek, who was picked up by dMC. Jiricek is a young defence prospect, and a manager cutting a young defenceman with some pedigree is taking a swing on patience. That makes two players dropped by Kegstands and picked up by a division rival in one week. In a league with a four-add weekly limit, handing the other teams free talent is an expensive habit.

Transaction Desk

by Dale Prychitko

The waiver wire was quiet this week. One bid, one team, $22. Sheeting Carlsson put that $22 on Jakub Dobes (G, MTL), 22 percent of the starting budget, and dropped Parker Kelly to make room. For a goalie on a Montreal team that has been rotating its netminders, that is a real price. In a format where a single start can swing twenty points either way, a goalie with a path to starts is a reasonable thing to spend on.

On the budget side, dMC has the lightest wallet in the division at $37 remaining, followed by armdawg at $48. Sheeting Carlsson sits at $71 after this week's bid and Hammer at $72. Slew Foot Slim has $89 left, Charles $94, MazerReed $97 and Walter White Walker $99.

Six teams are at the full $100: Beef, Corey's Crazy Team, Kegstands, zzz, HabsNation and I Walk for Sea Shanties. That is a lot of unspent money this early. The ones sitting on full budgets will have plenty of firepower when the right player hits the wire, and the managers who have already spent big, dMC and armdawg among them, will have to be choosier from here.

Expected Points

by Priya Raman

Walter White Walker had the best goaltending week in the division and still lost. Walter White Walker led all fourteen teams with 5 wins, 213 saves and 2 shutouts, and each of those figures was the sole top mark. The division average was 113.07 saves. The skaters let the goalies down: 3 goals and 22 points were the lowest in the division, and Hammer took the matchup 222.8-198.7 on 15 goals and 38 points. Goaltending is the biggest source of variance in this format, but a goalie week this good still needs skaters to back it up.

Corey's Crazy Team won the tightest matchup, 194.6-192.4 over Sheeting Carlsson, and it should not have been that close. Corey's Crazy Team allowed 18 goals against, tied with armdawg for the most in the division. It survived on 23 assists and 38 blocked shots. Sheeting Carlsson fired 102 shots to 87 and scored 11 goals to 9, and none of it was enough. A team winning with that goaltending is living on borrowed time.

armdawg is the best example of matchup luck this week. Its 217.0 points sat above the division average of 214.1, and it led all teams with 19 goals. It lost 278.1-217.0 to Beef, which topped the division in fantasy points (278.1), shots (122) and skater goals plus assists (46). Beef also allowed just 6 goals against, the fewest in the division, with only 86 saves needed. Saves are worth only 0.3 apiece, so the goaltending was about keeping pucks out, and Beef did it while the skaters piled on.

HabsNation went the other way. It led the division in hits with 63 and posted 133 saves, but managed only 55 shots on goal, the lowest total in the division, and lost 211.6-176.2 to dMC. dMC had the fewest assists in the division at 13 and still won on 18 goals, 47 hits and a division-high 45 blocked shots. That is the grinder's route to a win: a thin offence carried by the cheap stats. At the other end, I Walk for Sea Shanties posted the lowest total of the week, 168.3, on 75 saves and a single win.
