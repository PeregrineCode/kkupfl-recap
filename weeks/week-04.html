Week 4 Recap (2026-10-19 — 2026-10-25)

Tuesday, October 27, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Dale Prychitko

Corey's Crazy Team spent most of the week looking up at Slew Foot Slim and still finished on top, 229.2-213.6. Slew Foot Slim led from Wednesday through Friday, at one point sitting 20.4 points clear. Then Corey's Crazy Team swung back in over the weekend and was in front by Saturday. Mikko Rantanen led the roster with 29.0 points, with Macklin Celebrini at 25.0 and Ryan O'Reilly at 24.0 behind him. Slew Foot Slim got 30.3 from Logan Stankoven and 26.0 from Kirill Marchenko, and that wasn't enough to hold the lead. Losing a lead that late in the week is the kind that stings.

Charles beat I Walk for Sea Shanties 233.8-210.6. I Walk for Sea Shanties was in front on Wednesday, and Charles moved ahead by Thursday and kept the gap from there. Jason Robertson topped the Charles roster with 28.0 points, and Jack Roslovic added 25.8. Charles also got a shutout and a pile of saves from the crease, which covers a lot of sins elsewhere. David Pastrnak's 23.8 was the best of I Walk for Sea Shanties' week, and Charlie McAvoy chipped in 17.0 from the blue line.

MazerReed beat Walter White Walker 267.6-173.5, a 94.1-point margin and the widest on this week's slate. Leon Draisaitl alone put up 43.8 points, and Adam Fox added 27.8 from the back end. MazerReed also got five goalie wins. Walter White Walker had Nathan MacKinnon at 24.5 and Wyatt Johnston at 22.0, but the rest of the skaters managed little, and the team finished with a division-low 24 goals and assists.

armdawg beat Kegstands 268.8-204.4 and was in front every day from Wednesday on. Connor McDavid supplied 40.0 points, with William Nylander at 27.3 and Roope Hintz at 23.5. The shot volume was there as well, 117 on goal. Kegstands got a good week from Kirill Kaprizov (30.3) and Drake Batherson (29.5), and it still finished 64.4 points short.

dMC beat Sheeting Carlsson 276.2-217.6, and the win came from the skaters. Artemi Panarin led the roster with 33.8 points, Auston Matthews had 29.8 and Lucas Raymond 26.3, and the team put 137 shots on goal. That was with no goalie wins at all, so the forwards did nearly all the work. Sheeting Carlsson leaned on Kiefer Sherwood's 27.5 and Matt Boldy's 22.3, and the 172 saves its goalies made kept things respectable.

zzz took down HabsNation 211.8-186.5. The blue-collar points did it: zzz had 47 blocked shots and 31 assists, with Martin Necas (26.0) and Evan Bouchard (22.3) leading the way. HabsNation piled up 68 hits and got 30.8 from Artturi Lehkonen, but went winless in goal, and that's a hard week to recover from.

Hammer edged Beef 211.2-197.4, the closest margin of the week at 13.8 points. Easton Cowan led the Hammer roster with 25.3 points and Joel Hofer added 23.4 from the crease. Beef got 17.5 from Nikita Kucherov and 16.8 from Vincent Trocheck, but its goalies made just 17 saves on the week, and a net that quiet leaves a lot of points on the table.

Players of the Week

by Dale Prychitko

Leon Draisaitl of MazerReed takes the skater crown with 43.8 fantasy points, and he got there on volume. Six goals, three assists and 15 shots on goal, with a single hit to his name. The EDM centre has been shooting from every spot on the ice for years, and in this format every one of those shots pays 0.5 on top of whatever finds the net. Draisaitl is listed at C, LW and RW, which is the sort of flexibility a manager can't buy in a draft room.

The runners-up are a tight group. Connor McDavid of armdawg put up 40 points on two goals and eight assists, ten goals and assists in all, and his EDM linemate's six goals are the only reason he finished second. Artemi Panarin of dMC collected 33.8 points on four goals and 13 shots, a fine week from an LA winger. Kirill Kaprizov of Kegstands comes in fourth at 30.3 with three goals, three assists and 13 shots, a MIN scorer who doesn't mind a hit or a block along the way.

In goal, Joel Hofer of Hammer wins the week with 23.4 points, and his week was about workload. He faced 100 shots for STL, stopped 93 of them for a .930 save percentage and won twice, and the saves alone carried the load even with seven goals against. Charles got 20.7 from Arturs Silovs of PIT, who won twice and added a shutout. Jacob Fowler of MazerReed was the most efficient of the lot, a .964 save percentage on 55 shots for MTL with two wins and 18.9 points. Yaroslav Askarov of Sheeting Carlsson closed out the list at 18 points, stopping 70 of 74 for SJ in a single win.

Power Rankings

by Dale Prychitko

Contenders

Four teams have separated themselves from the pack, and three of them did it by scoring big rather than by squeaking through.

#1 dMC — 4-0-0 (1.000) — W, 276.2-217.6

The only unbeaten team in the division, and the week's top score belongs to dMC. The season points-for of 925.1 ranks third, behind armdawg and Beef, but a perfect record and the best weekly total earn the top spot.

#2 armdawg — 3-1-0 (.750) — W, 268.8-204.4

armdawg leads the division in season points-for at 953.5, and Armin's club handled Kegstands without much fuss. If the schedule had dealt a different hand, this could easily be the unbeaten team.

#3 Beef — 3-1-0 (.750) — L, 197.4-211.2

Beef lost a close one to Hammer, but 935.1 points for on the season says the team is strong and the loss was a bad week at the wrong time. Liam's group stays up here on the scoring.

#4 MazerReed — 3-1-0 (.750) — W, 267.6-173.5

A 94.1-point cushion over Walter White Walker makes this the most lopsided result I can find on the board. MazerReed has 908.2 points for and the second-lowest points-against of the contenders at 797.0, behind only dMC, so the record looks earned.

Solid

These three are in the playoff conversation, with records that hold up and scoring that mostly does too.

#5 Corey's Crazy Team — 3-1-0 (.750) — W, 229.2-213.6

Corey's Crazy Team edged Slew Foot Slim by a small margin and sits 3-1-0 on 841.5 points for. The record is a little ahead of the scoring, so I'd want to see another good week before moving it higher.

#6 Charles — 2-2-0 (.500) — W, 233.8-210.6

Charles beat I Walk for Sea Shanties and has 872.0 points for against 872.6 against, which is about as even as a season gets. The team is a true .500 in every sense.

#7 zzz — 2-2-0 (.500) — W, 211.8-186.5

zzz got past HabsNation to level its record at 2-2-0. The 837.8 points for is respectable, and Morgan's team is hovering right around the middle of the division.

Mediocre

The middle of the division is crowded, and a couple of these teams are better than their records suggest.

#8 Slew Foot Slim — 2-2-0 (.500) — L, 213.6-229.2

Slew Foot Slim has only 755.3 points against, the lowest figure I can see in the division, so the schedule has been kind. A 2-2-0 record on that soft a slate is not a flattering sign.

#9 Hammer — 2-2-0 (.500) — W, 211.2-197.4

Hammer took down Beef to get back to .500, though 795.8 points for is modest and 834.6 against is not helping. This one was a good result for a team that needs more of them.

#10 Kegstands — 2-2-0 (.500) — L, 204.4-268.8

Kegstands ran into armdawg at the wrong time. The 874.8 points against is the second-highest in the division, so some of the 2-2-0 record is bad luck rather than bad hockey.

#11 Sheeting Carlsson — 1-3-0 (.250) — L, 217.6-276.2

Sheeting Carlsson scored 217.6 and still lost to the week's highest total from dMC. That is a result worth shrugging at, though 772.6 points for on the season keeps it down here.

Rebuilding

The bottom of the table is where the season is already getting uncomfortable.

#12 HabsNation — 1-3-0 (.250) — L, 186.5-211.8

HabsNation has 742.4 points for and 788.7 against, so it is being outscored, and the 1-3-0 record fits. Nothing in the numbers suggests a quick fix.

#13 Walter White Walker — 0-4-0 (.000) — L, 173.5-267.6

Walter White Walker has faced 941.3 points against, the most in the division, which is real misfortune. Even so, 718.6 points for is thin, and this is a bad team that has also been unlucky.

#14 I Walk for Sea Shanties — 0-4-0 (.000) — L, 210.6-233.8

I Walk for Sea Shanties has 707.9 points for, the lowest in the division, and an 0-4-0 record to go with it. Matthew's team was competitive against Charles this week, which is the first sign of life.

Movers and Shakers

by Dale Prychitko

dMC sits alone at the top of the division at 4-0-0, climbing one spot to take the #1 seat. Perfect records are rare this early, and Dennis's club is the only one left without a blemish. armdawg moved up one to #2 at 3-1-0, while Beef slid two to #3 at the same 3-1-0 mark. Corey's Crazy Team also rose one to #5 at 3-1-0. With MazerReed at 3-1-0 as well, five teams sit on three wins or better, and Beef is the one heading the wrong way.

Slew Foot Slim and Kegstands took the hardest falls, each dropping three spots to #8 and #10 on identical 2-2-0 records, which says plenty about how tight this pack is. At the bottom, Sheeting Carlsson edged up one to #11 at 1-3-0, while HabsNation slipped to #12 with the same record. One good week in either direction shuffles half the division.

Best Pickup of the Week

by Dale Prychitko

Walter White Walker gets the nod this week, and the pickup is Arseny Gritsyuk of NJ. The LW/RW piled up 15 points on two goals, an assist, five shots on goal and two hits. A free-agent add that returns that kind of week is why managers keep checking the wire, and Walter White Walker earns the credit for it. The shot volume stands out. Five shots on goal is a steady stream of half-points that keep coming even when the puck doesn't go in.

Close behind is Joel Farabee of CGY, who gave Sheeting Carlsson 12.8 points on a goal, two assists, three shots, a hit and a block. Dev scooped him up and got a full, well-rounded line. Farabee's availability is its own story, and it's told elsewhere in this paper.

Third goes to Brayden Schenn of NYI, who gave Corey's Crazy Team 11.8 points. He had a goal, an assist, five shots and five hits, which is the hard-nosed, unglamorous kind of week this format rewards. A veteran centre who hits like that is worth a roster spot. Corey found a useful piece without spending a dime on it.

Hall of Shame

by Dale Prychitko

The headline this week belongs to armdawg and the Darcy Kuemper experiment. The LA goalie stopped six of seven shots for a .857 save percentage and no win, which comes to 0.3 fantasy points. Goalies are the biggest swing in the lineup, and armdawg got roughly nothing back from this one. Seven shots against with no decision is a week that never got going.

HabsNation gets a mention for Jake Neighbours of STL. The winger finished with 3 points on three shots, four hits and a block, and no goals or assists. That's a fair floor for a depth forward, but it's a hard pickup to defend when other wingers had weeks like Farabee's. HabsNation filled a lineup spot and little else.

The final entry is a little awkward, because Walter White Walker also owns this week's best pickup. Adam Edstrom of NSH gave Tony 4 points, built on ten hits, two shots and a block. Ten hits is a lot of contact for a total that small, so the hits did their job and the rest of the line did nothing. Tony still wins the week on the strength of the Gritsyuk add, but even a good waiver week can have one dud in it.

Goalie Stream of the Week

by Dale Prychitko

Philipp Grubauer of SEA takes the honour, streamed by MazerReed. He went 2-0 for 13.2 fantasy points, stopping 54 of 60 shots for a .900 save percentage. Six goals against drags the line down, and a shutout wasn't on offer. The 54 saves did most of the work at 16.2 points, with the two wins adding 6 more. MazerReed picked up a goalie who got a heavy workload and two wins, and that is the bet that pays.

Dan Vladar of PHI is the runner-up, streamed by Hammer. His line was cleaner, with 27 saves on 28 shots, one goal against and a .964 save percentage for 9.6 points. Even a second win would have left him short at 12.6. Efficiency is nice, but saves are worth points, and Grubauer faced twice as many shots.

MPR can be happy with a tidy start, and Jon with two wins. The difference between them came down to one extra victory, which is how streaming goes more often than not.

Front Office Failures

by Dale Prychitko

The worst sin in a daily-lineup league is sitting a player who is putting up numbers, and three teams did it. I Walk for Sea Shanties left James Hagens on the bench for two days he had stats, and he put up a goal, an assist, seven shots and three blocks for 12.5 points. That's a lot of production to leave on the pine. Slew Foot Slim did the same with Tyler Toffoli, who had a goal, an assist and four shots for 10 points over two benched days.

armdawg gets two entries. Jimmy Snuggerud and Evgeni Malkin each scored a goal and an assist while sitting on the bench, 8 points apiece on a single day. Pairing that with the Kuemper pickup, armdawg had a rough week at the controls. Malkin is the kind of veteran who rewards patience, and he sat out the day he scored.

Then there are the hot potatoes. Beef dropped Joel Farabee, and Sheeting Carlsson promptly picked him up and got 12.8 points out of him, so one team's loss was the other's gain. HabsNation dropped Mackenzie Blackwood and zzz grabbed him, which looks different once the FAAB bill comes in. And armdawg cut Tommy Novak, who landed with Kegstands. Churning the bottom of a roster is part of the game, but cutting a player who goes on to produce, as Farabee did, is how a manager ends up reading about it in this column.

Transaction Desk

by Dale Prychitko

The trade of the week sent Vasily Podkolzin (EDM) and Gabriel Landeskog (COL) to HabsNation, while armdawg received Cutter Gauthier of ANA. I like armdawg in this deal. Armin consolidated two roster spots into one, and a young centre with Gauthier's pedigree is the kind of piece that can swing weeks on his own. The open spot can go to the waiver wire, which is where this league's value tends to turn up. MikeJ gets two wingers with real names and a bit of upside, but in a points format I'd take the single higher-ceiling player and a free roster spot.

On the waiver side, the big bid was zzz putting $57, more than half the budget, on Mackenzie Blackwood of COL. That leaves Morgan with $43 for the rest of the season. Blackwood shows no stats in the data this week, so the spend returned nothing yet. A bid that size on a goalie is a real gamble in a format where one start can swing the week, and the payoff needs to come soon.

MazerReed was the model of restraint, bidding $3 on Tyson Foerster of PHI and dropping Chris Kreider. Foerster produced 10.8 points on a shorthanded goal, six shots, a hit and two blocks. That's an excellent return for the money, and Grubauer came from the same wire, so MazerReed had a good week of shopping.

The week's total was $60, most of it from one $57 bid. dMC has the lowest remaining budget at $37, with zzz ($43) and armdawg ($48) next. Sheeting Carlsson and Hammer sit in the low $70s, and most of the rest of the division still has $89 or more to spend. Those with deep pockets like Beef, Corey's Crazy Team, HabsNation and I Walk for Sea Shanties, all at $100, hold the leverage as the season goes on.

The Two-Phone Report

by Frankie "Two Phones" Lombardi

The biggest name on the board is Snuggerud. I'm told he's being shopped at rock-bottom prices, and a source passed that along on Wednesday. He sits on armdawg's roster, and the price says a lot. A manager who discounts a player this early has either lost faith in him or needs a roster spot for something else. Both explanations end with a buyer getting a bargain. Keep an eye on the bottom of the standings, where Walter White Walker and I Walk for Sea Shanties are both 0-4-0 and may be ready to move anything with a pulse.

Bolduc is the other name making calls. Thursday's word is that he's on the market, but the asking price has a lot of teams turning to other markets. I'm hearing the shoppers are going elsewhere, and a player nobody will meet the price on is a player whose manager will have to blink eventually. The 24-hour counter-offer window will punish anyone who overreaches, because a rival can swoop in with a better deal for the same player. My sources are watching that window closely.

Now the one with the best vantage point. A source tells me dMC has a log jam on defence, and requests for more favourable locations are coming from the blue line. At 4-0-0, dMC is the only unbeaten team in the division (armdawg, Beef, MazerReed and Corey's Crazy Team are all 3-1-0). A surplus on defence is a good problem, but it won't last, because every rebuilding team in the division knows exactly what dMC has too much of. Expect a package offer for a forward or a goalie before the week is out.

The recent deals tell me who's open for business. HabsNation took on Vasily Podkolzin and Gabriel Landeskog while armdawg came away with Cutter Gauthier. A 1-3-0 team making that kind of move is not sitting still. The waiver wire is moving too. zzz put down $57 of its FAAB on Mackenzie Blackwood, a serious bid for a goalie. MazerReed got Tyson Foerster for $3, which hardly dents the budget. That is the quiet kind of move I watch, because teams that spend small are usually saving for something bigger.

The February 17 deadline is still months away, but the phones are already busy. I'll have more when the other side picks up.

Expected Points

by Priya Raman

dMC's 276.2-217.6 win over Sheeting Carlsson came with no help from the crease. dMC led the division with 22 goals and 137 shots on goal, against a division average of 13.1 goals and 98.3 shots, and its goalies won zero games on 64 saves. Skaters carried the whole week. The 276.2 points were the highest score on the board, and the goalie slots barely contributed. It also leaves dMC exposed. A week with zero goalie wins could have gone the other way, and shot volume at this level rarely repeats.

Walter White Walker shows the opposite problem. It allowed 27 goals against, the most in the division and well above the 14.4 average, and lost 267.6-173.5 to MazerReed. The goalies won three games and made 174 saves, so the crease still came out about 21 points ahead after the goals against. The real gap was up front: the skaters added only 24 goals and assists, the division low, and MazerReed outscored them by about 77 points. MazerReed had the week's best win total with five, plus two shorthanded points, and its goalies netted about 38 points.

The cleanest goaltending contrast is Hammer against Beef. Hammer made 218 saves, the division high, but let in 24 goals, and still won 211.2-197.4. Beef allowed one goal all week, the division best, on only 17 saves and a single win. Its goalies simply weren't busy enough to bank save points, and one goal against on that little work earns little. At 0.3 per save, a quiet crease is a low-scoring one.

zzz won 211.8-186.5 with the division low in goals, at seven. It made up the difference with 31 assists, second only to armdawg, and 47 blocked shots, the division high. HabsNation put up 68 hits, the most in the division, and lost with zero goalie wins. Hits pay 0.25 each. A quarter-point stat can lead the league and still lose to a team stacked with assists and blocks, which pay more.
